2. What the conference abstract (Mitsuda & Kitahara 2017) tells us

  • NPP is a 1 km resolution distribution estimated with a carbon-balance-based stand growth model driven by climate values
  • The explanatory variables are concavity, the solar radiation index calculated from latitude/longitude, slope and aspect, and NPP
  • Data: Forest Resource Monitoring Survey (now the National Forest Inventory of Japan), 1 km climate factors, the GSI 50 m DEM
  • It does not state which species' model was used for NPP. The search range is not stated either

3. Calculation methods learned from Mitsuda & Ito (2015)

ElementCalculation method
VTEXFor each of the eight main compass directions, measure the depression angle to the lowest point within the search range, and sum them. Similar to Yokoyama's "negative openness" (Yokoyama et al. 2002)
SRAD (SRI in the paper)Proportional to the cosine of the solar incident angle. The incident angle is calculated from the solar zenith and azimuth angles and the slope and aspect of the surface (Smith et al. 1980). Whether the sun is above the surrounding skyline is determined using elevations within the search range
Search range100, 250, 500 and 1000 m were tested. For VTEX, the correlation with site index changed greatly with the search range, decreasing as the range widened. SRAD and upslope contributing area changed little

Implication for building abroad: it is now even more likely that both VTEX and SRAD can be made from a DEM in QGIS. Since VTEX is close to negative openness, existing openness tools such as those in SAGA may be usable as a substitute once the differences are checked (to be confirmed).
