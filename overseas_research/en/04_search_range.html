Search Range

What the search range in Mitsuda & Ito (2015) is and how it affects the plan ｜ Research following "Still unknown" #1 on page 3 ｜ 2026-10-19

Reference: Mitsuda, Y., Ito, S. (2015) J. For. Plann. 20: 45–51. Freely available on J-STAGE: J-STAGE

1. Definition

From the Introduction (p.46): algorithms that calculate topographic factors from raster DEMs require an arbitrary parameter (radius or window size) to define how far to search for the terrain that influences the phenomenon in the subject cell. The range (circle or rectangle) defined by that parameter is called the search range.

In other words, it is the distance out to which the surrounding terrain is examined when calculating a cell's value. Alongside grid resolution, it is positioned as another axis of "scale" in digital terrain analysis (citing Wilson et al. 2000).

2. How each element uses it (p.47)

ElementWhat the search range determines
SRI (= SRAD)Whether the sun, seen from the cell, is above the surrounding skyline (shading), judged from elevations within the range
UCA (upslope contributing area)The area within the range from which water flows into the cell (not included in ZoningKit)
VTEXFor each of eight directions, find the lowest point within the range and sum the depression angles to it

Ranges tested: 100, 250, 500 and 1000 m. DEMs: GSI 50 m (GSI50), GSI 10 m (GSI10), digitized contours (CNT). Resolutions: 10, 12.5, 25 and 50 m. Study site: Tano Forest Science Station, University of Miyazaki, 18 plots (site index from felling and stem analysis).

3. Results

  • VTEX: the wider the range, the worse the correlation with site index, for every DEM. The suitable range was "obviously 100 m". VTEX represents "ridgeness", and a wide range picks up valley bottoms beyond ridges, overestimating ridgeness
  • SRI and UCA: correlations quickly saturated as the range widened; the effect of the range was unclear

Combinations accepted in the best models (Table 2)

ModelSRIUCAVTEX
All data1000 m (CNT-10)100 m (CNT-12.5)100 m (GSI50-50)
GSI DEMs1000 m (GSI50-50)100 m (GSI10-10)100 m (GSI50-50)
GSI 50 m only (GSI50)1000 m (50 m)—100 m (50 m)
The Discussion text says "the search ranges of UCA, SRI and VTEX were 1000 m, 100 or 250 m, and 100 m, respectively", which reverses SRI and UCA relative to the tables. The tables are considered correct.

Fit with DEM resolution

  • UCA suits fine resolutions (10–12.5 m) of information-rich DEMs (CNT, GSI10), because groundwater flow is a fine-scale phenomenon
  • SRI suits a coarse resolution (50 m) of an information-poor DEM (GSI50). Solar radiation is a broad-scale phenomenon, and fine DEMs are "too detailed" to represent it
  • Coarse sources made finer (e.g., GSI50-10) and fine sources made coarser (e.g., CNT-50) were not useful. The resolution must match the quality of the source data

4. Implications for the plan

  1. Clue to ZoningKit's settings: ZoningKit is 50 m data made from the GSI 50 m DEM, so it may have been made with SRAD 1000 m and VTEX 100 m, as in this paper's "50 m only" best model (a guess). This can be checked by calculating VTEX from a local DEM and comparing with ZoningKit values
  2. Settings abroad: the paper says the effect of the range depends on the terrain of the site (the study site is highly undulating). Abroad, the search range should be a configurable plugin setting, with defaults of VTEX 100 m and SRAD 1000 m
  3. DEM resolution: even when using a 1 m LiDAR DEM abroad, SRAD may need to be calculated after coarsening. This affects the design
