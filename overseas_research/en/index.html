Discussion

The danger of treating the site index as correct (2026-10-19)

  • The abstract of Mitsuda & Kitahara (2017) says the relationship between site index and terrain "was clear for sugi and karamatsu, but unclear for hinoki". This shows in the coefficients: hinoki's solar radiation coefficient is 0.093, less than one tenth of sugi's (1.001) (page 2, section 0)
  • Whatever validation was done, since this concerns mountain forests, all of it is acceptable only as a mere index
  • For general users of the plugin, it is "dangerous". The output is named "site index", and its values, such as 18 or 21, look like tree heights in metres. The guide also says "a value of 18 means a tree height of 18 m at age 40", so it may be used as a correct value for decisions on planting species and silviculture
  • In reality it is only an estimate from a uniform nationwide formula, 1 km climate and 50 m terrain
  • Caveats in the guide and manual ("treat as a relative value") are not enough. How the output is presented is itself the countermeasure. Example directions (undecided): label it "site index (relative)", show it as a rank within the region (percentile), and state on screen when a species has weak support
  • Overseas, the Japanese formula or provisional coefficients would be used without local coefficients, making this problem even more serious. How to present the site index is one of the issues for the plan
