0. Plugin premise: how the site index works

Added 2026-10-19. Based on comparing the "Guide to Forest Zoning Considering Profitability and Disaster Risk" (Forestry Agency, revised March 2026, in Japanese; freely available from the G-Spatial Information Center: Forest Zoning Guide (R8.3 revision)) with the plugin code (processes/raster_writer/siteidx.py, profit.py)

The guide's formula differs from the code's formula

Formula in the MEMO on p.70 of the guide:

Sugi site index = 21.28 + 1.028*NPP − 1.001*SRAD − 1.111*VTEX

Formula the plugin actually calculates:

Sugi site index = 21.28 + (NPP − 15.82)×1.028 − (SRAD − 1347.09)×0.01×1.001 − (VTEX − 58.85)×0.01×1.111

The guide's formula omits subtracting a reference value from each input (15.82, 1347.09, 58.85) and the ×0.01. Calculated as written in the guide, with SRAD around 1400 the result would be something like −1400 m, which makes no sense. This is an issue with how the original guide is written.

How to read the formula

It has the form "tree height at an average site ± adjustments for conditions".

  • a (21.28 for sugi): the site index when NPP, SRAD and VTEX are at their reference values (b, d, f; probably the means of the fitting data). At an average site, about 21 m at age 40
  • Each term: adds for conditions better than the reference, subtracts for worse ones
  • ×0.01: likely a conversion to align the magnitude of SRAD and VTEX values (basis unconfirmed)

What the three elements are and why they matter

ElementWhat it isEffect on site indexRationale
NPP (net primary productivity)How much plants can grow in a year on that land, determined by climate alone: carbon absorbed by photosynthesis minus carbon released by respiration. Calculated on a 1 km grid by running Prof. Mitsuda's growth model with climate dataPositive (higher means better site)Trees grow better where it is warm and the air is not too dry. Represents broad-scale (regional) differences
SRAD (solar radiation index)How sunlight hits the surface, determined by slope aspect and steepness. Highest on steep south-facing slopesNegative (stronger sun means poorer site)Strong sunlight dries the soil. Sugi is sensitive to drought, so it grows better on shaded north-facing or valley slopes
VTEX (topographic exposure / concavity)How far the cell looks down on low points in the eight surrounding directions. High on ridges, low in valleysNegative (ridges mean poorer site)Ridges are wind-exposed and dry, and water does not collect there. Valleys collect water and stay moist

In short, the idea is to take "the land's base capacity determined by climate (NPP)" and subtract "susceptibility to drying (SRAD, VTEX)". The statement on p.16 of the guide that "topographic indices related to soil moisture, which strongly affects plant growth, … are highly correlated with site index" is the reason SRAD and VTEX are used.

Differences in coefficients between species

SpeciesNPP (climate)SRAD (sunlight)VTEX (ridge)Character it suggests

How much each element matters (estimate with zone VIII values, sugi)

Values obtained by putting the 1st–99th percentiles of the data (table on page 1) into the formula.

  • NPP: −4.5 m to +1.6 m (range about 6 m)
  • SRAD: +6.8 m to −3.1 m (range about 10 m)
  • VTEX: +0.7 m to −1.2 m (range about 2 m)

For sugi in zone VIII, sunlight (SRAD) has the largest effect.

How the plugin uses it

  1. Create site index rasters for the three species (siteidx.py)
  2. The user picks one species and sets two thresholds to divide it into scores of 1, 2 and 3 (profit.py)
  3. That score is added to the scores for "yarding efficiency" and "distance from roads" to give "profitability"
  4. Profitability and disaster risk are combined for zoning
The site index is used only to divide sites into scores; the height values themselves do not remain in the calculation. This is why the guide says to "treat it as a relative value": the design works as long as the ranking of sites is correct. Outside Japan, the formula need not be reproduced exactly; any indicator that correctly ranks site quality can be used.

2. Organizing the issues (Claude)

Current state of translation

Files using tr(): 0. Python files with hard-coded Japanese: 74; .ui files: 3; docs/manual.html is also in Japanese. There is no translation infrastructure yet.

Translation and localization are separate tasks

  • Translation: wrapping strings in tr() and preparing .ts/.qm files. Needed for any country and can proceed without waiting for the country choice
  • Localization: replacing site index, DEM, CRS and harvesting systems. Directly depends on the country choice and open data feasibility

Questions on whether open data really works

  1. Are the definitions known: how NPP, SRAD and VTEX are made (prerequisite)
  2. Licence: does the plugin fetch source data at run time, or do we host and distribute processed data
  3. Access: stable URLs, whether authentication is required, whether automated access is permitted (precedent: blocked by the Shizuoka Prefecture open data portal)
  4. Scientific validity: the gap between satellite canopy height and "dominant tree height". A trial in one region is needed

Proposed steps

  1. Decide the purpose and audience
  2. Confirm the definitions of the three elements
  3. Compare candidate countries
  4. Open data feasibility for each candidate country
  5. Trial in one region (translation infrastructure in parallel)

Definitions (from the papers)

For the meaning of each element and why it affects the site index, see 0. Plugin premise.

ElementDefinitionCan it be made abroad?
SRADSolar radiation index. Calculated from the solar zenith and azimuth angles and the slope and aspect of the surface (Smith et al. 1980). Sunlight intensity related to soil drying. 50 mYes From a DEM with the QGIS raster calculator
VTEXVertical exposure index. Sum of nadir angles in eight directions (Mitsuda et al. 2007). Susceptibility to wind-driven evapotranspiration. 50 mYes Search distance etc. to be confirmed in Mitsuda et al. 2007
NPPMean growth over 10 years simulated on a 1 km grid with a carbon-balance-based stand growth model, with uniform initial conditions. Climate 1971–2000. Higher where temperature is high and atmospheric dryness is lowNot the same thing Output of a model tuned to Japanese species

The fitting used the GSI 50 m DEM, the Japan Meteorological Agency 1 km gridded climate data, and the nationwide Japanese National Forest Inventory (NFI).

There is only one NPP raster, used in common for all three species. Which species' model was used must be confirmed in the abstract.

Conclusion: outside Japan, it will be rebuilt as "a climate factor replacing NPP (MODIS NPP 500 m, WorldClim temperature and dryness, etc.) + SRAD and VTEX + coefficients fitted locally". The form of the formula (a constant plus three linear terms) can be kept.

5. Ways to supply site index (design draft)

Three levels, assuming users prepare the data and the plugin guides or creates it.

LevelWhat the user preparesWhat the plugin doesSuited regions
A. Ready-made site indexA local site index rasterPoint to sources, align CRS and extentNew Zealand, British Columbia (Canada), etc.
B. Calculate from three elementsDEM, climate data (download guided)Automatically create SRAD and VTEX from the DEM. Coefficients are defaults or entered manuallyRegions without a ready-made site index
C. Fit coefficientsPlot CSV with height, age, species and coordinatesSample the three elements at plot locations and fit coefficientsUsers with survey data. Vegetation survey summaries come in here
  • The current plugin's "custom site index" input field is the basis for A. B and C are new
  • For licensing, the safest approach is not to bundle or relay data, but to guide users to obtain it themselves. Even non-commercial, no-derivatives data (Scion) remains usable by users themselves
  • Other replacements: CRS (UTM, etc.), DEM (global default Copernicus DEM 30 m), buildings and roads (OpenStreetMap), yarding efficiency (existing editor), reference age (configurable per species)
